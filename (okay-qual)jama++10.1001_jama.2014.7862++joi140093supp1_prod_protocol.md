# Statistical Analysis Plan

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale
Drug use is common, associated with health consequences, and often untreated. This primary-care trial evaluates screening plus two brief-intervention models because evidence for BI among adults identified through drug-use screening was described as lacking, despite existing screening tools and SBIRT dissemination initiatives. *(Source: Sections 8.0 and 12.1, pp. 6–7, 12–13)*

### 1.2 Objectives

**Primary Objectives**

- Determine whether enhanced BI (BI-E) and standard BI (BI-S), each compared with screening/resource information, decrease drug use among adults with ASSIST scores >=4. *(Source: Section 12.1, pp. 12–13)*

**Secondary Objectives**

- Determine effects on drug-use consequences including HIV-risk behaviors, and on receipt of substance-dependence treatment among participants with dependence. *(Source: Sections 12.1 and 14.4, pp. 12–13, 21–22)*

**Exploratory Objectives**

- Compare BI-E with BI-S; examine intervention effects among ASSIST 2–3 participants, additional drug outcomes and session mechanisms; and compare intervention implementation and net costs/outcomes. *(Source: Sections 12.1, 14.4, and 15.3, pp. 13, 22–23, 25–27)*

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **Design Type:** Investigator-initiated, single-center, psychosocial randomized controlled trial in BMC primary care, with baseline and 6-month assessment; there is no placebo group. *(Source: Sections 14.2 and 14.4, pp. 17, 20–21)*
- **Allocation Ratio:** Equal-probability 1:1:1 assignment after baseline assessment. *(Source: Section 14.2, p. 17)*
- **Intervention Summary:** All participants receive screening results and a written resource list.
  - BI-S: one health-promotion-advocate Brief Negotiated Interview based on motivational-interviewing principles.
  - BI-E: a 30–45-minute counselor-delivered motivational intervention, tailored using assessment/medical-record information, plus an optional phone or in-person booster within 1–2 months.
  - Information control: no BI at enrollment; control participants continuing drug use are referred for MASBIRT BI after the six-month assessment.
  - Up to 30 pilot participants are enrolled before the main study: up to 20 BI-E and 10 BI-S. *(Source: Sections 14.2 and 14.4, pp. 17–20)*

### 2.2 Randomization

- **Method:** Following baseline, staff use a web-based system that creates a unique study ID and returns allocation; assessment forms omit the randomized group, and post-baseline allocation is intended to preserve concealment during baseline interview. *(Source: Section 14.2, p. 17)*
- **Stratification Factors:** CIDI-SF-determined dependence, drug of greatest concern, and ASSIST category (2–3 versus >=4). *(Source: Section 14.2, p. 17)*
- **Minimization or Blocking:** Permuted blocks are used to balance numbers across groups. *(Source: Section 14.2, p. 17)*

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **Planned Sample Size:** 728 participants plus up to 30 pilots; at least 528 are expected to have ASSIST >=4. For that main-analysis stratum, 474 evaluable participants (158/arm) are expected at six months. *(Source: Section 15.1, pp. 23–24)*
- **Outcome Used for Calculation:** Six-month percent days use (PDU) in the past 30 days for the drug of greatest concern. *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 15.1, pp. 21, 24)*
- **Outcome Type:** Continuous percentage. **Protocol-internal discordance:** the calculation explicitly states a chi-square test with continuity correction, whereas this endpoint is PDU with SD 37 and the final primary analysis uses linear regression. The SAP does not reconcile or alter that source discrepancy; the test is retained below exactly as reported for the calculation. *(Source: Sections 14.4, 15.1, and 15.3, pp. 21, 24–26)*
- **Assumed Control-Arm Value:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pp. 1–52 reviewed)*
- **Assumed Treatment-Arm Value:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pp. 1–52 reviewed)*
- **Target Difference / Effect Size:** 14.7 percentage points absolute difference between control and each intervention. *(Source: Section 15.1, p. 24)*
- **Variability Assumption:** Overall six-month PDU SD 37, based on anticipated drug-of-concern mix: 40% marijuana, 31% cocaine, 20% opioids, and 9% other drugs. *(Source: Section 15.1, p. 24)*
- **Alpha (Significance Level):** Two-sided overall .05; for the calculation, .025 per each of two main control comparisons under Bonferroni. *(Source: Section 15.1, p. 24)*
- **Power:** 90%. *(Source: Section 15.1, p. 24)*
- **Statistical Test Assumed:** Chi-square test with continuity correction. *(Source: Section 15.1, p. 24)*
- **Required Number of Events:** Not applicable; the calculation is for a continuous PDU endpoint. *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 15.1, pp. 21, 24)*
- **Non-Inferiority / Equivalence Margin:** Not applicable; this is a superiority comparison. *(Source: Sections 12.1 and 15.1, pp. 12–13, 24)*
- **Dropout / Non-Adherence Inflation:** 10% loss to follow-up, yielding 474 evaluable main-stratum participants. *(Source: Section 15.1, p. 24)*
- **Design Effect / Clustering:** Not applicable; this is an individually randomized single-center trial. *(Source: Section 14.2, p. 17)*
- **Basis for Assumptions:** Babor marijuana and Bernstein heroin/cocaine studies; expected drug-of-concern distribution. *(Source: Section 15.1, p. 24)*
- **Sample Size Re-Estimation / Amendments:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pp. 1–52 reviewed)*
- **Software Used for Calculation:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pp. 1–52 reviewed)*
- **Sample Size Rationale:** The calculation targets a clinically important PDU difference; the protocol notes a 20-percentage-point absolute decrease in Babor et al. as context and separately enrolls pilots to test procedures. *(Source: Section 15.1, p. 24)*

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework
For ASSIST >=4 participants, the main superiority hypotheses test BI-E versus control and BI-S versus control for drug use, consequences/HIV risk, and dependent-participant treatment receipt. For the primary PDU analysis, those two BI-versus-control comparisons are primary; all pairwise comparisons are assessed secondarily using the Holm sequential procedure. BI-E versus BI-S, ASSIST 2–3 comparisons, session mechanisms, and costs are exploratory. *(Source: Sections 12.1 and 15.3, pp. 13, 25–27)*

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **Interim Analyses Planned:** No interim efficacy or futility analysis is reported; the PI monitors adverse events in real time and at weekly meetings. *(Source: Sections 17.0–17.6, pp. 29–30; pp. 1–52 reviewed)*
- **Stopping Guidelines:** There are no stopping rules. *(Source: Section 17.0, p. 29)*
- **Alpha Spending or Adjustment:** No alpha-spending approach is reported. *(Source: pp. 1–52 reviewed)*

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **Final Analysis Timing:** Recruitment was anticipated to take 36 months; the study began July 2008 and primary analyses were anticipated by May 2014. *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 23)*
- **Outcome Assessment Schedule:** Baseline, six-week phone brief assessment, and six-month in-person assessment; the final assessment has a window through 12 months after enrollment. Hair sampling occurs at entry and six months for non-incarcerated participants. *(Source: Section 14.4, pp. 20–21)*

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **Alpha Level:** Two-sided overall .05. *(Source: Section 15.1, p. 24)*
- **Multiplicity Adjustment:** For the primary PDU analysis, BI-E versus control and BI-S versus control are primary comparisons; all pairwise comparisons are assessed secondarily with Holm sequential adjustment. Bonferroni .025 per two main control comparisons is stated for power calculation only. *(Source: Sections 15.1 and 15.3, pp. 24–25)*
- **Confidence Intervals:** Exact binomial confidence intervals are reported around injection-drug-use proportions in each group; a general confidence level is not reported in protocol. *(Source: Section 15.3, p. 26; pp. 1–52 reviewed)*

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **Adherence Definition:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pp. 1–52 reviewed)*
- **Adherence Assessment:** Intervention sessions are audio-recorded; BI-E booster content is summarized by checklist, and 40% of recordings are coded using MISC for intervention characterization. *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 15.3, pp. 20–21, 26–27)*
- **Protocol Deviation Definition:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pp. 1–52 reviewed)*
- **Protocol Deviation Reporting:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pp. 1–52 reviewed)*

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **Intention-to-Treat:** All enrolled subjects are analyzed according to randomized assignment. *(Source: Section 15.3, p. 25)*
- **Per Protocol:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pp. 1–52 reviewed)*
- **Other Populations:** Main analyses are for ASSIST >=4; receipt-of-treatment is restricted to dependent participants. Exploratory analyses include ASSIST 2–3 and participants with baseline hair-confirmed drug use. *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 15.3, pp. 21–22, 25–27)*

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data
Two-stage screening begins with clinical past-3-month drug-use prescreening, then RA assessment of date, gender, age/year of birth, English fluency, pregnancy, capacity/willingness to return, and ability to provide two contacts; qualifying patients complete MASBIRT including ASSIST. Retained research screening forms have no identifier and describe enrolled and non-enrolled patients. *(Source: Sections 20.1 and 21.1, pp. 32–33)*

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary
Participants must be adults attending primary care, fluent in English, with ASSIST substance score >=2 and recent use of marijuana, cocaine, opioids, sedatives, meth/amphetamines, hallucinogens, inhalants, or other drugs; no MASBIRT intervention in the preceding three months; ability to return to BMC within six months; capacity to be interviewed by trained staff (excluding acute discomfort or significant cognitive impairment); and two contacts able to assist follow-up. Exclusions include pregnancy, OBOT participation, Suboxone/Subutex prescription, or expected Suboxone/Subutex induction within one week. *(Source: Sections 13.1–13.2, pp. 13–15)*

### 4.3 Recruitment
BMC MASBIRT health-promotion advocates identify potentially eligible primary-care patients and refer them to RAs; study screening and enrollment occur in private BMC clinic space. Pilot recruitment also permits calls to consented AHEAD-study participants. *(Source: Section 20.1, p. 32)*

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **Withdrawal Levels:** Participants may withdraw by telling study staff; information already collected remains in the study. *(Source: consent text, pp. 37, 40, 45)*
- **Withdrawal Data Collection:** No further assessment/testing is conducted after withdrawal; previously collected/tested data are maintained. For withdrawn hair-sample participants, remaining sample is retained under study ID unless authorization is revoked, when it is discarded. *(Source: Sections 26.1–26.2, pp. 51–52)*
- **Reasons for Withdrawal:** Reasons for withdrawal are not reported in protocol. Follow-up includes six-week contact and six-month assessment, with a 12-month final-visit window. Incarcerated participants may provide additional consent for facility/telephone assessment; hair testing is not done during incarceration. *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 22.1, pp. 20–21, 34; pp. 1–52 reviewed)*

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

- **Baseline Variables:** Baseline assessments include drug use and consequences, HIV risk, treatment utilization, health care utilization, health status, medical/psychiatric comorbidities and symptoms, demographics, heavy drinking, readiness to change, dependence criteria, and hair drug test. *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 15.3, pp. 21, 25–26)*
- **Summary Methods:** Descriptive statistics are calculated at baseline and six months; baseline arm differences use chi-square for categorical variables and ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis for continuous variables. *(Source: Section 15.3, p. 25)*

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes
**Outcome: Percent days use for drug of greatest concern** *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 21)*
- **Definition:** Percentage of days in the prior 30 days on which the participant used the drug identified at entry as of greatest concern. *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 21)*
- **Measurement Units:** Percent days use. *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 21)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Six months; the recall window is the preceding 30 days. *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 21)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Derived using Form 90/Timeline Followback calendar method; no transformation is reported. *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 21; pp. 1–52 reviewed)*

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes
**Outcome: Drug-use consequences (SIP-D)** *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 21)*
- **Definition:** Mean total Short Inventory of Problems–Drug score. *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 21)*
- **Measurement Units:** SIP-D total-score mean. *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 21)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Six months. *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 15.3, pp. 21, 26)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Total score; no transformation reported. *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 21; pp. 1–52 reviewed)*

**Outcome: HIV risk behavior—unsafe sex** *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 21)*
- **Definition:** Proportion reporting unsafe sex with a primary partner, other partner, or during exchange of sex for money or drugs, measured with RAB and published questions. *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 21)*
- **Measurement Units:** Proportion with unsafe sex. *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 21)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Six months, with baseline behavior included in analysis. *(Source: Section 15.3, p. 26)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Binary unsafe-sex outcome; no transformation reported. *(Source: Section 15.3, p. 26)*

**Outcome: Receipt of substance-dependence treatment** *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 22)*
- **Definition:** Among dependent participants, self-reported receipt of residential or outpatient treatment, counseling/therapy, medication, employee-assistance program, or mutual-help group. *(Source: Section 14.4, p. 22)*
- **Measurement Units:** Proportion receiving treatment. *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 15.3, pp. 22, 26)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Six months. *(Source: Section 15.3, p. 26)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Binary receipt outcome; no transformation reported. *(Source: Section 15.3, p. 26)*

**Outcome: Exploratory drug and intervention-process outcomes** *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 15.3, pp. 22–23, 26–27)*
- **Definition:** Self-reported 30-day abstinence; 90-day hair-test drug use/abstinence; Form 90D medium/heavy-use days; frequency/any use of any drug or risky alcohol; six-week self-reported abstinence, consequences and treatment receipt; and MISC-coded counselor/patient behaviors. Hair testing is qualitative and quantitative for marijuana, cocaine, opiates, PCP and amphetamines; prescription drugs are not detected, and the protocol states that quantitative clinical meaning is less clear across drugs. *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 15.3, pp. 22–23, 26–27)*
- **Measurement Units:** Binary, count/frequency, PDU, qualitative/quantitative hair testing, and coded session measures, as applicable. *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 15.3, pp. 22–23, 25–27)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Baseline and six months for hair; 30-day self-report and 90-day hair windows; six-week research assessment for early outcomes, distinct from the BI-E booster contact. *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 15.3, pp. 20–23, 26)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Hair measures cover a 90-day window; analysis uses any-drug-use binary outcome. Hair testing may be unavailable because of refusal, alopecia, or processing error. No other transformations are reported. *(Source: Sections 14.4 and 15.3, pp. 23, 26)*

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **Primary Analysis Methods:** In ITT ASSIST >=4 participants, linear regression models six-month PDU with treatment-arm indicators; primary comparisons are each BI arm versus control, and all pairwise comparisons are assessed secondarily with Holm adjustment. The overview specifies chi-square bivariate tests for categorical variables and ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis tests for continuous variables. Binary outcomes use logistic, counts Poisson, continuous outcomes linear, and nonnormally distributed continuous outcomes median regression. SIP-D uses linear regression with baseline SIP-D; unsafe sex and treatment receipt use logistic regression. Injection drug use is summarized by exact binomial CI, without planned between-arm hypothesis testing. *(Source: Section 15.3, pp. 25–26)*
- **Covariate Adjustment:** Models adjust factors not balanced by randomization. Candidate predictors include age, gender, race, dependence criteria, substance/heavy alcohol use, readiness, utilization, health status, and medical/psychiatric comorbidity/symptoms; unsafe-sex models adjust baseline unsafe sex. Exploratory correlations identify collinearity, and variables with r >0.5 are not entered together in multivariable models. *(Source: Section 15.3, pp. 25–26)*
- **Assumption Checks:** Baseline arm differences are assessed; exploratory correlations identify collinearity, and variables with r >0.5 are not included together. *(Source: Section 15.3, p. 25)*
- **Alternative Methods if Assumptions Fail:** Median regression is used for continuous outcomes that are not normally distributed. *(Source: Section 15.3, p. 25)*
- **Sensitivity Analyses:** Main analyses are repeated among participants with baseline hair-confirmed drug use; if informative missingness is evident, multiple-imputation and non-ignorable-nonresponse sensitivity analyses are considered. *(Source: Section 15.3, pp. 26–27)*
- **Subgroup Analyses:** Effect modification by readiness and dependence is assessed through stratification or interactions. Exploratory subgroups include gender, racial/ethnic groups, psychiatric symptoms, and concomitant unhealthy alcohol use; the protocol notes gender/race subgroup power may be inadequate. *(Source: Section 15.3, pp. 26–27)*

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **Missing Data Assumptions:** The sample-size calculation assumes 10% random, non-informative loss to follow-up. Informative missingness is assessed using comparisons of lost participants with completers and available utilization data. *(Source: Section 15.3, p. 27)*
- **Handling Methods:** Eligible enrollees versus eligible decliners are compared using two independent-samples t test and Fisher exact test, and analyses test lost-to-follow-up participants versus completers. Reasons for missingness are documented when possible. If evidence supports informative missing data, adjustments are considered, including sensitivity analyses with multiple imputation and non-ignorable nonresponse models. *(Source: Section 15.3, p. 27)*

### 5.5 Additional Analyses
Additional/exploratory analyses repeat primary/secondary analyses for BI-E versus BI-S and ASSIST 2–3 participants, analyze any drug use using logistic regression, assess six-week research-assessment outcomes for early differences (not the BI-E booster contact), and use 40% of intervention recordings coded with MISC to investigate counselor MI-consistent behavior, commitment talk, later drug use, and moderation by intervention. The exploratory economic objective compares implementation and net intervention costs/outcomes; net intervention cost includes implementation, future health care, and crime costs. Detailed cost analysis is deferred to the grant rather than this protocol. *(Source: Sections 12.1, 14.4, and 15.3, pp. 13, 20–23, 26–27)*

### 5.6 Harms

- **Adverse Event Definitions:** Potential harms include interview/randomization-related psychological stress, confidentiality breaches, and hair-sampling complications. The application characterizes this as a survey/interview/observational study, collecting/reporting only AE/SAE/UP related or possibly related to research; confidentiality breaches are reported as UPs. Detailed AE/SAE/UP definitions are in an attached protocol whose content is not included in this document. *(Source: Sections 16.1 and 17.2, pp. 27–29)*
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** The PI monitors events; staff report events as they occur, with real-time and weekly review involving the statistician, project manager, and co-investigators. Monitored events include interview stress, confidentiality breaches, and hair-sampling complications. Suicidal ideation/distress identified in clinical space prompts on-site physician/nurse/nurse-practitioner assessment; in research space, study physicians/psychologist are contacted, with referral as needed. *(Source: Sections 16.1–16.2 and 17.0–17.3, pp. 27–29)*
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pp. 1–52 reviewed)*
