5.6 Harms

Original

- **Adverse Event Definitions:** AE is any unfavourable medical event after study medicine regardless of dose/causality; ADR is harmful/unexpected drug response; SAE includes death, life threat, hospitalisation/prolongation, persistent/significant disability, congenital anomaly/birth defect, or other important medical condition; treatment-related SAE is SADR. *(Source: V5 §8.1; page 112)*
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** Safety endpoints are incidence of AEs and SAEs after medicinal-product administration, and insertional-mutagenesis risk determined by haematological analysis and clonality studies. Record all AEs in CRFs and assess causality (not related, improbable, possible, probable, certain, not evaluable); unrelated/improbable collection-related events are reported separately. Assess infusion toxicity, insertional-mutagenesis risk, and conditioning-associated risk. Bone-marrow lentiviral insertion analysis is optional; integration-site work uses LAM-PCR in FANCOLEN-1 or sonication/shearing, linker ligation, vector/linker PCR, Illumina MiSeqDX sequencing, hg38 alignment/annotation, customised R scripts and Excel in long-term follow-up. Shannon and Gini indices and UC50 characterise clonality/diversity. Record AEs/ADRs during treatment or within 30 days after the last dose. If rescue donor-cell therapy is required, after conditioning starts record only vector-infusion-related AEs; if a secondary neoplasm occurs, record its diagnosis as an SAE but not treatment-related AEs or complications. SAE reports are made within 24 hours; completed documentation within 10 days; unexpected SADRs within 15 days or 7 days if death. Follow each AE for at least one year. *(Source: Table S2; page 28; Supplementary Methods §1.2; pages 5–6; V5 §§8.1–8.4; pages 112–118)*
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** Use NCI-CTCAE v4.0 and determine the proportion with toxicity by grade. Toxicities not classified by that system use MedDRA categories mild/asymptomatic, moderate/symptomatic without significant functional impairment, severe/significant functional impairment, and life-threatening. The separate AE severity scale is minimal/mild (noticed but not affecting life), moderate (discomfort/affects daily life), severe (severely limits usual tasks and needs symptomatic treatment), and life-threatening (direct threat to life). The protocol additionally reproduces WHO acute/subacute grade 0–4 criteria for haemoglobin, leukocytes, granulocytes, platelets, bleeding, bilirubin, SGOT/SGPT, alkaline phosphatase, oral toxicity, nausea/vomiting, diarrhoea, BUN/urea, creatinine, proteinuria, and haematuria. *(Source: V5 §§8.1, 8.3.3; pages 112–114, 117)*

Regenerated

- **Adverse Event Definitions:** AE is any unfavourable medical event after study medicine regardless of causality; ADR is harmful/unexpected drug response; SAE is an event causing death, life threat, hospitalisation/prolongation, persistent/significant disability, congenital anomaly, or other important medical condition; a related SAE is SADR. Toxic death is death secondary to toxicity. *(Source: Protocol version 5 section 8.1, pages 112–113)*
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** Collect procedure-related AEs in screening and post-infusion AEs for three years; record AE/ADR occurring during treatment or within 30 days of the last dose. Determine proportion with toxicity by grade. Record all AEs in CRFs, report unrelated/improbable events separately, fax SAEs within 24 hours, submit completed SAE documentation within 10 days, and follow AEs at least one year. After donor rescue, collect only vector-infusion-related AEs; for secondary neoplasm, record only the diagnosis, not treatment/complication effects. *(Source: Protocol version 5 sections 8.1–8.4, pages 112–118)*
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** Grade with NCI-CTCAE v4.0, including protocol table thresholds for haematologic, gastrointestinal and renal/vesical toxicities; use MedDRA mild/moderate/severe/life-threatening categories if unclassifiable. Investigator causality categories are not related, improbable, possible, probable, certain and not evaluable; severity categories are minimal/mild (noticed, no life effect), moderate (discomfort/daily-life effect), severe (limits usual tasks/requires symptomatic treatment), and life-threatening.
