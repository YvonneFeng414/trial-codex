# Statistical Analysis Plan

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

This single-centre study evaluates whether fluids as tolerated (FAT), using apple juice diluted 1:1 with water, are no worse than standard Pediatric Electrolyte Solution (EMS) for low-risk children aged 6 months to 5 years with mild gastroenteritis, vomiting, or diarrhea; EMS is the control. *(Source: eAppendix 1, page 3; eTable 1, page 11)*

### 1.2 Objectives

**Primary Objectives**

- Determine whether FAT using half-strength apple juice is no worse than standard EMS in the described low-risk pediatric gastroenteritis population; the article title identifies treatment failure. *(Source: eAppendix 1, page 3; Supplementary Online Content, page 1)*

**Secondary Objectives**

Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-16 reviewed)*

**Exploratory Objectives**

Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-16 reviewed)*

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **Design Type:** Single-centre randomized, controlled, initially double-blind study. RSP/Central Pharmacy personnel preparing kits are unblinded; study staff and families are blinded in the ED, and caregivers receive an allocation-specific unblinding envelope at discharge. *(Source: eAppendix 1, pages 3, 5-6)*
- **Allocation Ratio:** 1:1 FAT treatment arm to EMS control arm. *(Source: eAppendix 1, page 3)*
- **Intervention Summary:** Blinded study solution is supplied as 2 L and used at 10 mL/kg per watery stool and 2 mL/kg per emesis until resolution. *(Source: eAppendix 1, page 4)*
  - FAT: apple juice diluted 1:1 with sterile water; after the supplied fluid, any fluids as tolerated may be used if more is needed. The solution is color-matched to EMS with food colouring. *(Source: eAppendix 1, pages 4-5, 7)*
  - EMS control: Pediatric Electrolyte Solution, apple flavor; after the supplied fluid, EMS is encouraged if more is needed. Both study solutions are dispensed in 500-mL white opaque labeled bottles, supporting ED blinding. *(Source: eAppendix 1, pages 3-5, 8)*

### 2.2 Randomization

- **Method:** RSP prepared a master randomization table and sequentially numbered pre-kits incorporating treatment allocation; study staff log the participant and assigned kit number. *(Source: eAppendix 1, pages 3-4)*
- **Stratification Factors:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-16 reviewed)*
- **Minimization or Blocking:** Random block sizes are reported; minimization is not reported in protocol. *(Source: eAppendix 1, page 3; pages 1-16 reviewed)*

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

Trained triage nurses assign the Clinical Dehydration Scale. Of 872 approached children represented in the consent tables, 647 consented and 225 declined. Declining reasons were lack of interest (134; 60%), dislike of apple juice (24; 11%), inability/unwillingness for follow-up (17; 8%), preference for EMS (14; 6%), parental fatigue (12; 5%), breastfed only (12; 5%), unwillingness to administer EMS (6; 3%), preference for IV rehydration (3; 1%), and other (3; 1%). *(Source: eTable 1, page 11; eTable 2, page 12; eTable 3, page 13)*

For consented and consent-declining children, the supplied table reports age, ondansetron administration, index-visit IV rehydration and hospitalization, and unscheduled ED visit; the ethics board did not permit additional data collection from decliners. *(Source: eTable 3, page 13)*

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

Inclusion criteria were: *(Source: eTable 1, page 11)*

- Ontario residence; age 6-60 months; presentation to The Hospital for Sick Children ED. *(Source: eTable 1, page 11)*
- At least 3 vomiting or diarrhea episodes in the preceding 24 hours, onset under 96 hours, and weight at least 8 kg. Vomiting is forceful expulsion of stomach contents; diarrhea is watery stool able to take the shape of a container. *(Source: eTable 1, page 11)*
- Clinical Dehydration Scale below 5 (score 0-4, none-some dehydration), capillary refill under 2 seconds, and eligibility to begin oral rehydration at triage. *(Source: eTable 1, page 11)*
- No bulging fontanelle, bilious vomiting, blood in diarrhea/vomit, non-periumbilical abdominal pain, abdominal distension, acute disease requiring alternate treatment, or significant coexisting disease. *(Source: eTable 1, page 11)*

Exclusion criteria were chronic gastrointestinal or other disease increasing treatment-failure risk; prematurity with corrected postnatal age under 30 weeks; history of bilious vomiting, hematemesis, hematochezia, or clinical concern for acute abdomen; physician judgment that immediate IV rehydration was needed; or an insurmountable language barrier to consent or follow-up. *(Source: eTable 1, page 11)*

### 4.3 Recruitment

Recruitment was planned from 8 AM to midnight, Monday to Sunday, 6 days per week but variably. *(Source: eAppendix 1, page 3)*

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**Outcome: Treatment failure** *(Source: Supplementary Online Content, page 1)*

- **Definition:** In the ED, crossover from EMS to FAT or FAT to EMS after an alternate solution is provided is classified as treatment failure. A complete treatment-failure definition is not reported in protocol. *(Source: eAppendix 1, page 6; pages 1-16 reviewed)*
- **Measurement Units:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-16 reviewed)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** ED crossover is recorded while the participant is in the ED; the assessment window for complete treatment failure is not reported. *(Source: eAppendix 1, page 6; pages 1-16 reviewed)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Crossover is recorded, the alternate solution is documented, and EMS-to-FAT or FAT-to-EMS crossover is classified as treatment failure. Breastfeeding is not considered a crossover. No other derivation is reported. *(Source: eAppendix 1, page 6; eTable 4, page 14)*

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

The supplied material reports the Figure 2 logistic model: log-odds = theta0 + theta1×group + theta2×age + theta3×ln(age) + theta4×group×age + theta5×group×ln(age), yielding OR(age) = exp{theta1 + theta4×age + theta5×ln(age)}.
