# Statistical Analysis Plan

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

This Phase III trial evaluates whether daily oral tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF) or co-formulated emtricitabine/TDF (FTC/TDF) prevents HIV-1 acquisition and is safe in HIV-1-uninfected partners in heterosexual HIV-1-discordant couples receiving prevention services. *(Source: Study Summary and Sections 4.5, 6.1; pages 15-18, 36-40)*

### 1.2 Objectives

**Primary Objectives**

- Determine whether once-daily oral TDF or FTC/TDF provides additional protection against HIV-1 acquisition. *(Source: Sections 3, 5.1; pages 15, 37)*
- Compare adverse-event (AE) rates for TDF and FTC/TDF with placebo. *(Source: Sections 3, 5.1; pages 15, 37)*

**Secondary Objectives**

- Assess efficacy by exposure, gender, index-partner CD4/viral load, HSV-2, sexually transmitted infections (STIs), and circumcision. *(Source: Sections 3, 5.2; pages 15-16, 38)*
- Assess adherence, its association with efficacy, and drug sharing; characterize sexual-behavior change; assess congenital abnormalities and infant growth after in-utero exposure; and assess seroconverter viral load, CD4, resistance, and other disease parameters. *(Source: Sections 3, 5.2; pages 15-16, 38-39)*

**Exploratory Objectives**

Use stored specimens to evaluate immunogenetic and virologic determinants of transmission, including viral phenotype/genotype, co-receptor usage, innate immune polymorphisms, HLA match, and other host genetic factors. *(Source: Sections 3, 5.3; pages 16-17, 39)*

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **Design Type:** Phase III, multisite, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, three-arm, endpoint-driven trial. The later placebo-conversion phase followed disclosure of efficacy, discontinued placebo, and offered consenting placebo participants 12 months of blinded active PrEP. *(Source: Sections 6.1, 14.1; pages 39-40, 79; Protocol Addendum #8; pages 181-186)*
- **Allocation Ratio:** 1:1:1 for original TDF, FTC/TDF, and placebo arms; the placebo-conversion phase allocates TDF versus FTC/TDF with equal chance. *(Source: Sections 3, 6.1; pages 17-18, 39-40; Protocol Addendum #8; pages 182-184)*
- **Intervention Summary:** Two oral tablets are taken once daily: TDF arm, active TDF 300 mg plus placebo FTC/TDF; FTC/TDF arm, placebo TDF plus active FTC/TDF 200 mg/300 mg; placebo arm, both placebos. Active/placebo versions were indistinguishable. *(Source: Sections 3, 6.1; pages 17-18, 39-40)*

### 2.2 Randomization

- **Method:** The Protocol Biostatistician/designee generates and maintains the code; participants receive sequential IDs, manufacturer-filled sequentially numbered bottles, and six-month kits assigned through computer or telephone randomization. *(Source: Section 14.2; pages 79-80)*
- **Stratification Factors:** The final primary Cox model is stratified by site. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.6.1; page 232)*
- **Minimization or Blocking:** Variable-size block randomization is used. *(Source: Section 14.2; page 79)*

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **Planned Sample Size:** 4,700 HIV-1-seronegative partner participants/couples, 1,566 per arm, after Addendum #6 increased the original 3,900/1,300 plan. *(Source: Protocol Addendum #6; pages 177-179; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0; page 222)*
- **Outcome Used for Calculation:** Confirmed HIV-1 seroconversion/acquisition endpoint. *(Source: Sections 14.4-14.5; pages 81-83; Protocol Addendum #6; page 177)*
- **Outcome Type:** Time-to-event HIV-1 acquisition. *(Source: Sections 14.4-14.5; pages 81-84)*
- **Assumed Control-Arm Value:** Original calculations used placebo incidence of 3%-4%; the amendment used 2.8 per 100 person-years. *(Source: Section 14.4; pages 81-82; Protocol Addendum #6; page 177)*
- **Assumed Treatment-Arm Value:** True efficacy was assumed to be 60% for each active arm versus placebo. *(Source: Section 14.4; page 81)*
- **Target Difference / Effect Size:** Each active arm is powered to distinguish 60% efficacy from the minimum worthwhile/null efficacy of 30%; pregnancy/off-drug time was incorporated as effective 57% efficacy. *(Source: Section 14.4; pages 81-82)*
- **Variability Assumption:** Not reported in protocol; the calculation is event-driven survival analysis rather than a continuous-outcome variance calculation. *(Source: pages 1-243 reviewed; Sections 14.4-14.5; pages 81-84)*
- **Alpha (Significance Level):** Standard type-I error 0.05; the detailed final plan specifies one-sided α=.025 for the 30%-efficacy Wald test. *(Source: Section 14.4; page 81; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.6.1; page 232)*
- **Power:** 80% for 60% versus 30% efficacy for each active-versus-placebo comparison; 90% to detect 61%-65% efficacy under stated incidence assumptions and 90% for combined 60% treatment effect in original calculations. *(Source: Section 14.4; pages 81-82)*
- **Statistical Test Assumed:** Cox proportional-hazards/log-rank comparison of each active arm with placebo; the detailed plan uses a Wald test for HR=.70 versus HR<.70. *(Source: Sections 14.5.1, 14.5.4; pages 82-84; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.6.1; page 232)*
- **Required Number of Events:** 191 total HIV endpoints, corresponding to 147 for each active-versus-placebo pairwise comparison. *(Source: Section 14.4; page 81; Protocol Addendum #6; page 177)*
- **Non-Inferiority / Equivalence Margin:** Not applicable: the efficacy comparisons are active arm versus placebo, not non-inferiority or equivalence comparisons. *(Source: pages 1-243 reviewed; Sections 14.4-14.5; pages 81-84)*
- **Dropout / Non-Adherence Inflation:** Approximately 18% loss to follow-up (7% yearly) was anticipated. Pregnancy/breastfeeding was expected to account for ~10% of female study time, or 5% of total study time, and was included in the original calculation. *(Source: Section 14.4; pages 81-82)*
- **Design Effect / Clustering:** Not applicable: this is not a cluster-randomized design. *(Source: pages 1-243 reviewed; Sections 6.1, 14.2; pages 39-40, 79)*
- **Basis for Assumptions:** Placebo incidence assumptions were based on Partners in Prevention HSV/HIV trial experience; the amendment adopted its observed 2.8/100 person-year placebo incidence. *(Source: Section 14.4; page 82; Protocol Addendum #6; page 177)*
- **Sample Size Re-Estimation / Amendments:** DSMB may recommend adjustment if incidence or follow-up differs. Addendum #6 increased N to 4,700 after the 2.8/100 person-year estimate. *(Source: Section 14.5.5; page 87; Protocol Addendum #6; pages 177-179)*
- **Software Used for Calculation:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-243 reviewed)*
- **Sample Size Rationale:** The endpoint-driven design uses a common placebo arm and separately tests both active regimens, to rule out efficacy below 30% while targeting public-health-relevant protection. *(Source: Section 14.4; pages 81-82)*

For the distinct co-primary safety objective, the original calculation states that 1,300 participants per active arm would yield a 95% CI of 0%-0.2% for an AE rate when no events are observed. *(Source: Section 14.4; page 82)*

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

Two formal active-versus-placebo efficacy comparisons are planned, without multiple-comparison adjustment. The primary scientific literature question tests 0% efficacy with a two-sided .05 site-stratified log-rank test; the design hypothesis tests H0: HR=.70 against HA: HR<.70 with a one-sided .025 Wald test, reflecting the 30% efficacy threshold. *(Source: Sections 14.4-14.5; pages 81-84; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.6.1; page 232)*

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **Interim Analyses Planned:** DSMB reviews safety, conduct, and scientific validity about every six months, with one earlier safety/conduct review, and anticipated 2-3 scheduled efficacy reviews around 30, 60, and 90 of the expected 122 endpoints across three arms. The embedded final SAP expects four DSMB efficacy meetings in the proposed 52-month study. *(Source: Section 14.5.5; pages 86-87; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, Interim Analysis Plan; page 239)*
- **Stopping Guidelines:** Before implementation, the DSMB charter is to define stopping, unblinding, unscheduled triggers, voting, and futility procedures; scenarios include efficacy, AE excess, external evidence, low incidence/adherence, and loss to follow-up. *(Source: Section 14.5.5; pages 87-88)*
- **Alpha Spending or Adjustment:** HIV-incidence interim analyses use an O'Brien-Fleming stopping rule. *(Source: Section 14.5.5; page 87)*

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **Final Analysis Timing:** Participants receive study drug for 24-36 months, followed one month after drug discontinuation for delayed endpoint identification; seroconverters have at least 12 months of follow-up. The embedded final SAP estimates 30-month accrual and about 52 months total study duration plus ~12 months to finish infant/seroconverter procedures. DSMB arm-specific tables are confined to closed reports, with arms labelled A/B/C and data current to about two months before each formal meeting. *(Source: Sections 6.1, 14.1; pages 39-40, 79; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0; pages 222-225, 239)*
- **Outcome Assessment Schedule:** Partner HIV testing and pregnancy testing are monthly; index follow-up is quarterly; partner chemistries/CBC occur at screening, Month 1, and quarterly, with monthly behavioral/symptom/adherence assessments. *(Source: Sections 8.3, 9.3, 12.1; pages 54-66, 69-72; Appendix I-II; pages 108-114)*

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **Alpha Level:** All tests are two-sided unless otherwise specified; the primary 0%-efficacy test is two-sided .05, while the 30%-efficacy test is one-sided .025. *(Source: Section 14.5.4; page 84; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.6.1; page 232)*
- **Multiplicity Adjustment:** No multiple-comparison adjustment is performed for the two efficacy comparisons. The protocol says safety analyses with several AEs will use multiple-comparison adjustment, while the detailed safety plan uses nominal p values to guide observed active-placebo excesses. *(Source: Sections 14.5.1-14.5.2; pages 82-83; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.8.1; page 236)*
- **Confidence Intervals:** Primary results report HRs and 95% CIs; robust variance methods form CIs in repeated-observation analyses. *(Source: Sections 14.5.3-14.5.4; pages 83-86; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.6.1; page 232)*

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **Adherence Definition:** The protocol per-protocol analysis requires reported adherence of at least 90%; the detailed SAP distinguishes adherence (proportion taken when dispensed) from coverage (estimated days exposed from dispensing and pill counts). *(Source: Section 14.5.1; page 83; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.3; page 227)*
- **Adherence Assessment:** Self-report, returned-bottle pill count, dispensing records, and index-partner drug assays assess adherence/sharing. *(Source: Section 14.5.3; pages 83-84; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.3 and A.9.1; pages 227-229, 237)*
- **Protocol Deviation Definition:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-243 reviewed)*
- **Protocol Deviation Reporting:** A general deviation taxonomy is not reported. However, the final SAP requires a detailed explanation of every exclusion: safety-significant eligibility violations exclude efficacy analyses; invalid consent excludes the participant; double enrollment excludes all data; and site-EIA-positive participants are censored for efficacy while their seroconverter-follow-up safety data continue. *(Source: pages 1-243 reviewed; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.1; pages 225-226)*

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **Intention-to-Treat:** Eligible randomized partner participants comprise the ITT cohort; original protocol efficacy includes randomized participants receiving any study drug, irrespective of adherence, excluding enrollment-PCR-positive participants. *(Source: Sections 14.5.1, 14.5.4; pages 82-84; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.1; page 225)*
- **Per Protocol:** Participants reporting ≥90% adherence over follow-up comprise the protocol per-protocol analysis; the detailed coverage analysis is an exploratory per-protocol analysis based on dispensing/pill-count coverage. *(Source: Section 14.5.1; page 83; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.7.1; pages 233-235)*
- **Other Populations:** Primary efficacy modified ITT excludes HIV-RNA-positive participants at randomization; as-treated efficacy excludes no-drug intervals. Safety uses ITT cohort with primary as-treated events on drug/within four weeks, with pregnancy exceptions. Index, infected/seroconverter, and placebo-conversion cohorts are also specified. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.1, A.8-A.9; pages 225, 236-239; Protocol Addendum #8; pages 181-187)*

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

Screening uses independent consent and stepwise assessment of demographics, behavior, locator information, medical history/examination, parallel rapid HIV tests, renal/hepatic/hematologic function, HBV, and eligibility. Baseline summaries include age, education, employment/income, children, CD4/viral load/WHO stage for index participants, circumcision, gender, marital status, living-together duration, sexual practices, and contraception. *(Source: Sections 6.2, 8.1, 9.1; pages 40-45, 51-53, 60-61; Appendix I-II; pages 108-114; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.2; pages 226-227)*

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

Partner participants are adults aged 18-65 in an eligible heterosexual discordant couple, sexually active with the partner (≥6 vaginal acts in three months), HIV-negative at screening and enrollment, able to provide locator information, and HBsAg-negative. Renal eligibility requires Cockcroft creatinine clearance ≥60 mL/min and creatinine ≤1.3 mg/dL (men) or ≤1.1 mg/dL (women); hepatic eligibility is bilirubin ≤1.5× upper limit of normal and ALT/AST <2× upper limit; hematologic eligibility is ANC >1,300/mm³, platelets >125,000/mm³, and hemoglobin >11 g/dL, within 56 days. One repeat screen ≥28 days later is allowed for initially failing renal/hepatic/hematologic criteria. Pregnancy/planned pregnancy, breastfeeding, relevant competing trials, relocation/travel >2 consecutive months, repeated ≥1+ glycosuria/proteinuria, serious illness, selected medications, and investigator-determined safety/interpretability issues exclude participation. Index participants must be HIV-positive by EIA, have CD4 ≥250 cells/mm³, no ART indication/current ART or clinical AIDS diagnosis, and meet couple/consent/locator requirements. *(Source: Sections 6.2.2-6.2.3; pages 41-45)*

### 4.3 Recruitment

Recruitment includes VCT centers, antenatal/PMTCT programs, HIV-care referrals, community promotion and outreach, other research projects, and health/social-service providers. Both partners independently consent and undergo stepwise screening; specified prior Partners-in-Prevention participants have visit-performance requirements and a three-month break before screening. *(Source: Sections 6.2.1, 6.3; pages 40-47)*

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **Withdrawal Levels:** Participants may withdraw study drug while continuing study follow-up, or withdraw from study; investigator/sponsor may discontinue a participant or study. Drug is stopped for detected HIV infection, pregnancy/breastfeeding, and clinically indicated toxicity. *(Source: Sections 6.6, 8.8, 10-11, 12.5; pages 48, 59-60, 66-68, 73)*
- **Withdrawal Data Collection:** A stop visit and post-study-drug follow-up identify delayed infection; early exits are asked to complete final procedures. The detailed SAP censors efficacy at seroconversion and retains seroconverter AE data. *(Source: Sections 8.4-8.5, 9.4-9.5; pages 56-58, 65; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.1; pages 225-226)*
- **Reasons for Withdrawal:** Reasons include participant choice, pregnancy/breastfeeding, HIV infection, toxicity/clinical safety, prohibited medication, inability to attend/complete procedures, study cancellation, and investigator judgment; relationship dissolution does not itself end follow-up. *(Source: Sections 6.6, 8.8, 12.5, 14.5.4; pages 48, 59-60, 73, 84; Appendix X; pages 146-148)*

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

- **Baseline Variables:** Participant/index demographic, clinical, and behavioral variables listed in Section 4.1 are summarized, including baseline sexual behavior with study/non-study partners and contraceptive use. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.2; pages 226-227)*
- **Summary Methods:** Use statistics appropriate to measurement scale, overall and by site/arm (and gender where specified). The original protocol says statistical testing will assess potential randomization-arm differences in adherence/behavioral descriptions, whereas the later final SAP specifies no formal testing for accrual or baseline demographic/behavioral tables. *(Source: Section 14.5.1; page 83; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.2; pages 226-227)*

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**Outcome: Incident HIV-1 infection** *(Source: Section 14.3; pages 80-81)*

- **Definition:** Serologically confirmed incident infection; HIV RNA >50 copies/mL at the last study visit, including Stop-visit RNA-positive/post-drug seroconverters; or other endpoint-committee-determined incident infection. Enrollment RNA positivity in first-three-month seropositives indicates pre-randomization infection and excludes efficacy analysis. *(Source: Section 14.3; pages 80-81)*
- **Measurement Units:** Binary confirmed infection event; incidence is events per person-year. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.6.1; page 232)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Monthly rapid HIV tests; time is randomization to first confirmed positive test for cases and last on-study negative test otherwise, unless endpoint committee assigns another visit. *(Source: Sections 6.1, 14.5.1; pages 40, 82; Appendix I; pages 108-110; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.6.1; page 232)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Efficacy is derived from hazard ratio versus placebo; primary efficacy modified ITT excludes RNA-positive-at-randomization participants. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.1, A.6.1; pages 225, 231-232)*

**Outcome: Co-primary safety—adverse events** *(Source: Sections 5.1, 14.5.2; pages 37, 83)*

- **Definition:** Any untoward medical occurrence after investigational product, irrespective of causality; clinical, laboratory, serious, expedited, and Grade 3/4 events are assessed. *(Source: Section 12.4; pages 72-73; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.8; pages 236-237)*
- **Measurement Units:** AE rate and event counts by treatment arm; primary detailed safety outcomes are serious, expedited, and Grade 3/4 laboratory events. *(Source: Sections 14.5.2; page 83; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.8; pages 236-237)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Partner AEs are monitored from randomization through final study visit including post-follow-up; primary detailed safety events include onset within 56 days of last dispensing. *(Source: Section 12.4; page 73; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.8.1; page 236)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Primary safety dataset includes on-drug and four-week post-drug events; pregnancy losses/congenital abnormalities from pregnancies detected on medication are retained regardless of interval. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.1, A.8.1; pages 225, 236)*

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**Outcome: Adherence, drug coverage, and drug sharing** *(Source: Sections 5.2.2, 14.5.3; pages 38, 83-84)*

- **Definition:** Adherence is pills taken when dispensed; coverage estimates days exposed between HIV tests; sharing is self-reported or assessed by index drug assays. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.3; pages 227-229)*
- **Measurement Units:** Monthly proportion of pills taken, self-reported missed doses/multiple missed doses, dispensing rates, coverage categories, and any sharing. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.3, A.9.1; pages 227-229, 237)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Monthly partner visits with returned-bottle pill count and questionnaires. *(Source: Appendix I; pages 108-110)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Coverage is 0% if no drug is dispensed, unknown if dispensed without pill count, otherwise pills not returned divided by expected doses. Descriptive adherence/coverage tables use 0%, 1-79%, 80-90%, and ≥90%; the supportive treatment-effect model uses 0%, 1-75%, 76-89%, ≥90%, and missing. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.3, A.7.1; pages 228, 234-235)*

**Outcome: Sexual risk behavior and efficacy cofactors** *(Source: Sections 5.2.1, 5.2.3; page 38)*

- **Definition:** Sexual frequency, condom use, non-primary partners, and exposure/cofactors including index viral load/CD4, HSV-2, STIs, gender, and circumcision. *(Source: Sections 14.3, 14.5.3; pages 81, 83-84)*
- **Measurement Units:** Number of acts, unprotected sex, any non-primary partner, and median-based total/unprotected act categories. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.9.2; page 238)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Enrollment and follow-up behavioral assessments; STI assessment at enrollment and yearly, with symptom ascertainment quarterly and clinically indicated assessments. *(Source: Appendix I; pages 108-111)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Four behavioral displays are any non-primary partner, any/none unprotected sex, and below/at-or-above median total and unprotected sex acts. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.9.2; page 238)*

**Outcome: Infant congenital abnormalities and growth after in-utero study-drug exposure** *(Source: Sections 5.2.4, 6.1; pages 38, 40)*

- **Definition:** Assess congenital abnormalities and growth in infants born to HIV-uninfected female participants who become pregnant; study drug is stopped when pregnancy is detected. *(Source: Sections 5.2.4, 11; pages 38, 67-68)*
- **Measurement Units:** Clinical teratogenicity assessment, growth parameters, and early renal function; precise derived metrics are not reported in protocol. *(Source: Section 6.1; page 40)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Infants are assessed soon after birth and quarterly for one year; the DSMB specifically reviews in-utero exposure safety. *(Source: Section 6.1; page 40; Section 14.5.5; page 87)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-243 reviewed)*

**Outcome: Early HIV-1 disease among infected cohort** *(Source: Sections 5.2.5, 14.5.3; pages 38-39, 84)*

- **Definition:** Viral set point, CD4 trajectory, and TDF/FTC-associated mutations among seroconverters and baseline-RNA-positive participants. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.9.3; pages 238-239)*
- **Measurement Units:** Plasma viral load, CD4 cell count, and proportions with K65R, K70E, M184V/I, any, or none mutations. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.9.3; pages 238-239)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Seroconverter visits occur at detection, <1 month, then quarterly ≥12 months; set-point viral load uses observations 4-24 months after estimated infection and excludes post-ART values. *(Source: Appendix III; pages 115-117; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.9.3; page 238)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** RNA-positive/antibody-negative first evidence is dated 17 days earlier; antibody-based infection is midpoint of last negative/first positive. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.9.3; page 238)*

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **Primary Analysis Methods:** Site-stratified Cox proportional-hazards model (SAS PROC PHREG), randomization arm as sole covariate, Efron ties; report incidence, HR, 95% CI, and comparisons of TDF/placebo and FTC/TDF/placebo. Safety rates use exact Poisson-rate tests in the protocol. Longitudinal sexual frequency and condom use use Poisson and binomial GEE models for correlated data; adherence uses logistic regression/GEE with robust variance for repeated observations. *(Source: Sections 14.5.1-14.5.3; pages 82-84; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.6.1, A.9.1; pages 232, 237-238)*
- **Covariate Adjustment:** Primary model has arm only. Supportive coverage analyses adjust for gender, age, site, baseline STI/behavior, time-dependent behavior, circumcision, HSV-2, and index viral load/CD4; cofactor models assess univariate then multivariable significant terms. *(Source: Section 14.5.1; page 83; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.7.1-A.7.3; pages 234-236)*
- **Assumption Checks:** Standard model-checking methods assess linear trend in adherence; nonlinear CD4 trajectories are considered if linearity fits poorly. *(Source: Sections 14.5.4; pages 85-86; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.9.1-A.9.3; pages 237-239)*
- **Alternative Methods if Assumptions Fail:** Seroconverter continuous outcomes use nonparametric tests, or t-tests when centrally distributed; categorical outcomes use chi-square or Fisher exact tests when a treatment arm has <10 seroconverters. *(Source: Section 14.5.3; page 84)*
- **Sensitivity Analyses:** Supportive efficacy analyses censor pregnancy/breastfeeding off-drug time, then all known no-drug intervals; coverage interaction analyses and covariate-adjusted HRs are specified. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.7.1; pages 233-235)*
- **Subgroup Analyses:** Evaluate treatment effects by gender, male partner circumcision, high/low index viral load, region, index circumcision for female partners, sexual activity, age, baseline CD4, HSV-2, STI, and contraception; report incidence by arm and subgroup. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.7.2; page 235)*

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **Missing Data Assumptions:** No general missing-data assumption or imputation model is reported. The pregnancy/breastfeeding supportive analysis explicitly invokes uninformative censoring of pregnancy time; coverage may be unknown when pill count is unavailable. *(Source: pages 1-243 reviewed; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.7.1; pages 233-235)*
- **Handling Methods:** No imputation is reported. Nonseroconverters are followed to last on-study negative HIV test; unconfirmed suspected infection is censored at last negative visit; participants who die or decline further testing are uninformatively censored at last valid test in coverage analysis. Off-drug time is excluded only in specified supportive/as-treated analyses. *(Source: Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.6.1-A.7.1; pages 231-235)*

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

Additional work includes a per-contact infectivity generalized linear model for HIV serostatus over each partnership testing interval, with counts of intercourse types and covariates including treatment, condom use, circumcision, HSV-2, index CD4/viral load, ART, relationship duration, and STIs; robust variance accounts for repeated intervals. Other work includes descriptive/regression analyses of adherence and risk behavior; the separately enumerated comparison of behavioral changes in former Partners-in-Prevention HSV/HIV Transmission Trial couples when infected versus uninfected partners receive study drug; infant congenital-abnormality/growth assessment; index drug-sharing/ART-initiation summaries; archived-specimen secondary/tertiary analyses; and placebo-arm conversion analyses after efficacy disclosure. *(Source: Sections 5.2, 13, 14.5.3-14.5.4; pages 38, 78-79, 83-86; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.3-A.9; pages 227-239; Protocol Addendum #8; pages 181-187)*

### 5.6 Harms

- **Adverse Event Definitions:** An AE is any untoward medical occurrence temporally associated with investigational product, whether or not causal; it includes unfavorable signs, symptoms, disease, and abnormal laboratory findings. *(Source: Section 12.4; pages 72-73)*
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** Compare AEs by arm using exact tests for Poisson rates; detailed safety analyses summarize serious, expedited, and Grade 3/4 laboratory events in as-treated and all-event safety datasets. *(Source: Section 14.5.2; page 83; Statistical Analysis Plan Version 2.0, A.8; pages 236-237)*
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** DAIDS adult/pediatric AE grading applies, with protocol-specific creatinine thresholds; licensed clinicians determine grade and relationship (unrelated through definitely related). EAEs include death, life-threatening/Grade 4, hospitalization, disability, all Grade 3, fetal loss, congenital anomaly, Grade 2 creatinine, and fracture, and are reported within 48 hours. *(Source: Sections 12.2, 12.4-12.8; pages 72-75)*

Social harms—loss of privacy, stigma, relationship difficulties, physical/verbal abuse, employment interference, and coercion—are solicited quarterly, recorded on CRFs, and referred to the site lead clinician/counselor. *(Source: Section 12.6; page 73)*
