SAP QC Pipeline

How a generated SAP markdown is checked against its protocol PDF without sending whole PDFs to a judge. Every SAP field cites its source pages, so the check works claim by claim. It asks two questions. Coverage: does retrieval reach the cited source pages? Accuracy: are the claims right? Accuracy is measured two ways: on every claim a set of risk checks flags, and on a random 30% of all claims.

Protocol PDF + SAP .md→ Page text→ Atomic facts→ Top-k retrieval→ Coverage · Recall@k→ Triggers→ Judge every flagged fact+ Judge a random 30%→ Finding-error acc · Random acc

One document, step by step

1
Deterministic · tools/extract_pdf.py

Page text that matches the citations

Reuse the generator's page-tagged text (===== PAGE N OF M =====), so a citation's "page N" and the PDF page index are the same number by construction. If text from another extractor is used, estimate the offset δ first: the most common value of (top-1 retrieved page − cited page) over claims that contain numbers.

  • OCR any page with too little text (the same rule as UNSUPPORTED_SCAN)
  • Mark table-of-contents pages. They are left out of retrieval and keyword search
UNSEARCHABLE · pages with no text even after OCR. A "not reported" fact that depends on them becomes unverifiable, not consistent
2
Deterministic, plus one cheap model call per SAP

Parse citations, split into atomic facts

  • For each block, *(Source: …)* gives the cited pages S(c). Section numbers like "1.1–1.3" are not read as pages, and sweeps like "pages 1–96 reviewed" are kept apart from the specific pages
  • Split compound claims into atomic facts (item, value, status). An eligibility block, for example, becomes one fact per criterion
  • A fact is an absence claim when its status is absent, however it is worded ("Not reported", "no other …", "not applicable")
3
Deterministic · no model tokens

Rank pages: Topk(c)

The query is the SAP heading plus the fact text. All pages are ranked by three scores, combined by reciprocal rank fusion. The cited pages are not used in the ranking.

  • BM25 over whole pages: exact terms, drug names, method names
  • Dense: 250-word chunks, page score = best chunk
  • Number match: |N(c) ∩ N(ti)| / |N(c)|, numbers normalized (0.05 = 5%)
  • RRF(i) = Σr 1/(60 + rankr(i)). Tied pages get their average rank, and IDF is fitted on PDF text only

Evidence packet for the judge = at least one passage from every cited page, plus the Topk passages, with duplicate passages removed across the document.

route · for PDFs of about 100 pages or fewer, one long-context call with the whole PDF and every fact can cost the same as retrieval. In that case judge every fact and skip sampling
4
Metric 1 · coverage

Source Recall@k: does retrieval reach the cited source?

Recall@k = (1/|𝒞|) Σc∈𝒞 𝟙[ S(c) ∩ Topk(c) ≠ ∅ ]
  • Chooses k: the smallest k with Recall@k ≥ 0.95, computed on facts whose page the judge confirmed. Then P(flagged unsupported | actually supported) ≤ 1 − Recall@k
  • Flags per fact: no cited page in Topk → a coverage miss, which becomes a trigger
  • Report it next to the random-pick baseline 1 − C(n−|S|, k)/C(n, k) and split by |S|, because a citation of many pages hits easily

It measures topic, not correctness. With 58 claims corrupted (numbers scaled, TLH↔TAH, ≥↔≤), Recall@5 only fell from 0.97 to 0.91.

coverage miss · sent to the judge with the whole cited page added
5
Routing · two independent selections, each fact judged at most once

Flagged by triggers, plus a random 30%

Flagged T · every fact judged

  • A number is missing from the cited page, or the comparison (≥/≤), one- vs two-sided, or arm (TLH/TAH) disagrees
  • Coverage miss, or the only citation is a page sweep
  • Absence fact with candidate pages
  • The summary contradicts itself: a field says absent, but the same outcome block states it
  • Low word overlap with the best passage from its cited pages
  • Compound fact (more than 3 items), or a critical section: randomization, sample size, interim analyses, analysis populations, primary outcome, primary analysis, missing data

Random 30% R · drawn from all facts

  • Systematic sample over facts in section order: a random start u, then every 1/0.3 positions. Every fact has inclusion probability 0.3, and sections are covered in proportion to their size
  • Don't draw ⌈0.3·Nh⌉ per section. With 23 sections of 1–30 claims, that takes 39%, not 30%
  • The draw doesn't depend on the triggers, so it estimates the whole document's accuracy
  • A fact in both T and R is judged once, and its verdict counts in both
  • Sampled facts that aren't flagged (R ∩ U) measure what the triggers miss
  • Fix the seed and record it with the results
T · finding-error accuracy
R (30%) · random accuracy
cost · facts judged = |T ∪ R|, roughly 0.3·N + 0.7·|T|
6
Judge model · a different model from the generator and the reviewer

Verdict with a quote that a script checks

  • Verdict: supported contradicted not found. A supported or contradicted verdict must include a verbatim quote
  • A script confirms that the quote appears on a page the judge was shown. A quote that isn't there means the verdict is rejected
  • Fixed checks on numbers, comparison direction, negation and arm can overrule the judge, or send the fact to human review
  • Absence facts: the candidates are pages matching a fixed term list for that heading, plus pages with BM25 above a calibrated threshold, plus the top-k pages by embedding similarity. The judge is asked "Does any passage specify ⟨item⟩? Quote it." With no candidates, the fact counts as consistent without a judge call
contradicted · counted as an error, with the quote attached
not found · an error, at most 1 − Recall@k of which are retrieval misses
7
Report, per document and pooled

Two accuracies, plus the trigger miss rate

See the definitions below. Report them by section as well, because an error in the sample size or primary analysis matters more than one in the background section. Pool across documents with a random effect for document.

output · <id>.qc.csv with one row per fact: verdict, quote, cited pages, Topk, trigger(s), stratum

The metrics

Coverage

Source Recall@k

P̂( rankc(S) ≤ k )

Says whether retrieval reaches the cited pages, and how large k must be.

Doesn't say whether a claim or its citation is correct.

Accuracy on flagged facts

Finding-error accuracy

accT = #supportedT / |T|

Says how accurate the flagged facts are. Every flagged fact is judged, so accT has no sampling error. Error yield = 1 − accT shows whether the triggers find errors efficiently.

Doesn't say how accurate the document is. Flagged facts are risky by design.

Accuracy on a random 30% of all facts

Random accuracy

accR = #supportedR / |R|,  πi = 0.3

Says how accurate the whole document is, without bias. Every inclusion probability is equal, so it's a plain proportion. The errors in R ∩ U give the trigger miss rate.

Doesn't say much for one document on its own: about 33 facts per SAP gives a wide interval, so pool across documents.

QuantityEstimateUncertainty
Document accuracy accR (no combination needed) Clopper–Pearson interval, by section if needed. With n ≈ 0.3·N ≈ 33 facts and 0 errors, the one-sided 95% error bound is 1 − 0.051/n ≈ 8.7%. Pooled over 100 SAPs (about 3,300 facts), a document random effect gives a much tighter interval
Lower-variance option ( |T|·accT + |U|·accR∩U ) / N Same data, split by flagged vs unflagged. T is a census, so all of the variance comes from R ∩ U
Trigger miss rate #errors(R∩U) / |R∩U| Small counts per document, so read it pooled. Showing it is below 2% takes about 150 unflagged facts with no errors (≈ 3/n)

Validating the judge (before the full run)

Planted errors give labels for free

Corrupt about 100 facts: scale the numbers, swap TLH↔TAH, flip ≥↔≤, change ITT to per-protocol, and relabel a reported item "Not reported". Judge them blind, mixed with the originals.

  • Detection rate = P(judge says not supported | corrupted)
  • False-alarm rate = P(judge says not supported | original, correct)

Human gold labels

About 100 facts labeled by hand across documents and sections. Report agreement with the judge (Cohen's κ). Planted errors don't look like real ones (C96 below is a real one), so they don't replace this.

Pilot · 10.1001_jama.2017.2068 (LACE)

One document, 111 claims, 96 PDF pages. The pilot's triggers were the six critical sections, coverage misses and absence claims, so T has 37 claims. R is a systematic 30% draw over the claims in section order (seed 2026), so R has 33 claims across 16 of 23 sections. 54 claims were judged (|T ∪ R|). The verdicts came from Claude in the same session, with no measured judge error rate, so they show how the pipeline works rather than giving reliable numbers.
